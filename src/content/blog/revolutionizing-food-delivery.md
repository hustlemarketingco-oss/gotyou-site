<p><strong>The Hidden Costs and How GOTYOU Can Transform Your Restaurant</strong></p>

<p>Food delivery apps have revolutionized how we order food, bringing convenience right to our doorsteps. But beneath this convenience lies a hidden cost, and many restaurateurs are starting to question, “Do delivery apps hurt restaurants?” and “Why do delivery apps hurt local restaurants?”</p>

<p>Today, we’re going to explore the challenges posed by third-party ordering services like UberEats, GrubHub, and DoorDash and why embracing a companion app like GOTYOU can drive long-term benefits for your business.</p>

<h3>The Problem with Third-Party Ordering Services</h3>

<h4>1. High Commission Fees</h4>

<p>Third-party ordering services charge exorbitant commission fees, ranging from 15% to 30%. For restaurants, already navigating razor-thin profit margins, these fees can be crippling.</p>

<h4>2. Loss of Brand Identity</h4>

<p>When customers order through third-party apps, they interact with the app’s brand, not yours. This erodes your brand identity and the unique experience your restaurant offers.</p>

<h4>3. Less Control Over Customer Experience</h4>

<p>With third-party services, you lose control over the customer journey. Your food’s presentation and delivery are in the hands of others, often leading to subpar experiences.</p>

<h4>4. Data Ownership Issues</h4>

<p>Third-party platforms often withhold valuable customer data. This data is the lifeblood of understanding your customers and refining your offerings.</p>

<h4>5. Reduced Customer Loyalty</h4>

<p>The multitude of options on third-party platforms makes it challenging to build customer loyalty. Customers are just a click away from ordering elsewhere.</p>

<h4>6. Reliance on External Platforms</h4>

<p>Relying on third-party services is a precarious position. Changes in commission structures, priorities, or even shutdowns can jeopardize your business.</p>

<h4>7. Data Utilization for Third-Party Benefit</h4>

<p>These services collect data across millions of consumer purchases. A business may introduce a customer to DoorDash and then have to pay a fee to get them back. Meanwhile, all of this data is used in DoorDash&#8217;s AI engine to optimize their revenue, not yours. This means your customer data is working against you, enhancing the third-party service’s profits rather than supporting your small business.</p>
