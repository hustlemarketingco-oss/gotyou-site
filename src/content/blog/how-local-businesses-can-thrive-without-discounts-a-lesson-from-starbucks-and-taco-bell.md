<p>The days of relying solely on discounts to attract customers may be coming to an end. As Starbucks and Taco Bell shift away from discount-heavy strategies, local businesses should take note and consider alternative ways to build lasting relationships with their customers. But how can smaller businesses—without the marketing budgets of Starbucks or Taco Bell—compete in this changing landscape? The answer lies in platforms like GOTYOU, which empower local merchants to level the playing field through personalized engagement and rewards, without depending on constant discounts.</p>

<h3>Starbucks’ Shift: Moving Away from Discounts to Build Loyalty</h3>

<p>Starbucks has taken a bold step by reducing promotions through its mobile app and focusing on elevating the in-store experience. CEO Brian Niccol’s vision is to return to the brand’s roots as a community coffeehouse, where customers pay full price for their coffee and enjoy a premium experience. This move allows Starbucks to reduce strain on its employees, improve profitability, and cultivate a loyal customer base that values more than just a deal.</p>

<p>Local businesses can learn from this approach. Discounting might bring customers through the door, but it often attracts deal-seekers who won’t return unless there’s another offer. Instead, creating a personalized and memorable experience can foster real loyalty.</p>

<h3>Taco Bell: Gamification and VIP Access</h3>

<p>Taco Bell is another brand setting an example. Its loyalty program goes beyond discounts, offering unique experiences and gamified rewards to engage customers. From early access to products to exclusive VIP events, Taco Bell has shown that customers want more than just discounts—they want to feel connected to the brand.</p>

<p>By rewarding customers with experiences and engagement, Taco Bell encourages higher spending and repeat visits. Loyalty customers spend 40% more annually than other guests. This is a strategy local businesses can easily adapt—by offering rewards for loyalty rather than discounts, businesses can encourage customers to keep coming back.</p>
