<p>Today is Cyber Monday. This is the day built for clicks, not conversations. The day that rewards speed instead of presence. The day that turns shopping into something that resembles a reflex more than a choice.</p>

<p>Cyber Monday was invented to push ecommerce forward at a time when home internet speeds were slow and online shopping still needed encouragement. In 2005, merchants wanted people to try ordering from websites. Today, they want us to order without even thinking.</p>

<p>But here’s the paradox of 2025:</p>

<p>We’re more connected digitally than ever, and yet our connection to the places we live has thinned.</p>

<p>On Cyber Monday, millions of people will scroll through flash sales, hop between tabs, follow price trackers, and chase algorithmic deals. But almost none of that activity strengthens their community.</p>

<p>A dollar spent on a major ecommerce platform rarely touches local soil. It moves through logistics warehouses, corporate payroll, and shareholder returns. It jumps over the city you’re standing in.</p>

<p>After spending the last few days watching local businesses fight for attention—in Savannah, Ocala, Knoxville, and beyond—it’s hard not to notice the contrast.</p>

<p>Small Business Saturday is about showing up.<br>Cyber Monday is about checking out.</p>

<p>Small Business Saturday is about relationships.<br>Cyber Monday is about transactions.</p>

<p>This isn’t an anti-online argument. Online shopping serves real purpose: accessibility for people with mobility issues, convenience for parents juggling households, availability of items not found locally. It’s a tool.</p>

<p>But the truth is simple:</p>

<p>When you shop locally, you strengthen the place where you live.<br>When you shop online, you strengthen the place where someone else lives.</p>

<p>If Friday was about frenzy and Saturday about intention, then maybe today should be about reflection.</p>

<p>Before clicking “Buy Now” ask:</p>

<p>Is there a local shop that sells this?<br>Is there a local service that can provide it?<br>Is there a human nearby who can answer a question about it?</p>

<p>Because if Cyber Monday vanished tomorrow, we would adjust. We’d still have stores. We’d still have human transactions. We’d still have Main Streets.</p>

<p>But if Main Street vanished tomorrow, no amount of same-day shipping could replace it.</p>

<p>So today, even on a day named after the internet, consider a hybrid approach:</p>

<p>Buy some things online if it makes sense.<br>But try—purposefully—to steer some spending toward local shops this week.</p>

<p>Because Cyber Monday was built for efficiency.<br>But community was built for resilience.</p>

<p>As we move toward the close of 2025 and into 2026, the question quietly persists:</p>

<p>Are we building a world optimized for convenience at the cost of connection?</p>

<p>Or can we build one where both coexist—and where technology finally supports local commerce rather than swallowing it?</p>

<p>This season, support local where you can. Support real places. Support real people. Because the strength of a community isn’t measured by the speed of delivery…</p>

<p>It’s measured by the depth of belonging.</p>
