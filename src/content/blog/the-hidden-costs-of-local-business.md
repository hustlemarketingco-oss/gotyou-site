<h3>Empowering Communities with GOTYOU</h3>

<p>It’s the day before Christmas—a season of giving, community, and reflection. For local businesses like restaurants and retail shops, this time of year is critical. The <a href="https://www.entrepreneur.com/growing-a-business/how-small-businesses-can-capture-76-billion-holiday-spend/483796">holiday season</a> often determines whether they can thrive in the coming year or struggle to stay afloat. The stakes are high, and the challenges are many. Yet, in the midst of this, technology offers an opportunity to rewrite the story for local commerce.</p>

<h4>The Cost of Competing in Local Markets</h4>

<p>Local businesses collectively invest <a href="https://www.emarketer.com/content/local-ad-spending-will-hit-175-6-billion-2024-thanks-political-boost">$175.6</a> billion annually in marketing to attract customers, competing not just with each other but also with national and global brands. While this spending drives awareness, it often benefits big tech companies more than the businesses themselves. Consider these facts:</p>

<ul>
<li>Nearly 50% of all Google searches have local intent.</li>

<li>98% of consumers use the internet to find local businesses.</li>

<li>76% of consumers read online reviews regularly when deciding where to shop or dine.</li>
</ul>

<p>The reliance on platforms like Google and Facebook, which charge high fees for visibility, creates a financial burden. While these tools help businesses gain exposure, the majority of the revenue leaves the community and flows into the coffers of big tech.</p>

<h4>The Downside of Deep Discounts</h4>

<p>Over the years, platforms like Groupon have tempted local businesses with promises of new customers through deep discounts. While the initial traffic surge can be appealing, the long-term effects are often damaging. These platforms take significant commissions, leaving businesses with razor-thin margins. Additionally, they attract deal-seekers who are unlikely to become loyal customers.</p>

<p>Take a restaurant offering a $20 voucher for $12 through Groupon. The platform takes a significant cut, often leaving the business with less than $6—hardly enough to cover costs, let alone generate a profit. This model is unsustainable for most small businesses.</p>

<p>In the near future, imagine the same restaurant offering a $20 voucher through GOTYOU. With GOTYOU’s model, the restaurant prices the voucher at $17. The consumer uses 50 GET tokens (equivalent to $5), meaning they still pay only $12 out of pocket. After GOTYOU’s modest 10% fee of the retail value (just $2), the restaurant retains $15—an incredible 150% increase compared to the $6 they would make with Groupon.</p>

<p>This isn’t just an incremental improvement; it’s a complete game-changer. GOTYOU uses cutting-edge technology, including blockchain and NFTs, to redefine the way businesses and consumers interact. Here’s how it goes beyond dynamic discounting:</p>
