<h3>Why We&#8217;re Taking GOTYOU on the Road This Summer</h3>

<p>Walk down any Main Street in America, and you’ll see it.</p>

<p>Local businesses — the heartbeat of our communities — struggling just to stay afloat. Storefronts are closing, signs are fading, and the hopeful energy that once defined these neighborhoods is starting to slip away.</p>

<p>At the center of this problem is something that was supposed to be a solution: <strong>advertising</strong>.</p>

<h3>Advertising Was Meant to Help Small Businesses Grow</h3>

<p>Now It’s Slowly Draining Them.</p>

<p>For decades, advertising has been sold as the lifeblood of business growth. Get your name out there, bring in new customers, and let the profits follow — that was the promise.</p>

<p>But the reality for most small businesses in 2025 couldn’t be further from that.</p>

<p>Local advertising has become a <strong>$171 billion</strong> industry in the U.S., and it’s dominated by digital giants like <strong>Google</strong> and <strong>Meta</strong> (Facebook, Instagram). These companies rake in the lion’s share of that money, yet offer <strong>very little return</strong> for local merchants trying to drive real foot traffic or build meaningful customer relationships.</p>

<h3>What’s Broken?</h3>

<p>Here’s what we’re hearing from small businesses across the country:</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Ad costs are skyrocketing</strong>, often 20–30% higher year-over-year</li>

<li><strong>Attribution is nonexistent</strong> — they have no idea what works</li>

<li><strong>Customer data is locked up</strong> by the platforms they paid to advertise on</li>

<li><strong>They’re competing with national brands</strong> using the same channels</li>

<li><strong>They feel like they’re just burning money</strong>, hoping for results that never come</li>
</ul>

<p>We’ve spoken to hundreds of business owners, and the story is nearly always the same:</p>

<blockquote>
<p>“We’re doing everything we can… and it still feels like we’re invisible.”</p>
</blockquote>
