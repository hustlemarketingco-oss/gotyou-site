<p>Last week, I was enjoying lunch at a charming sandwich shop in a strip center near John Wayne Airport in Orange County, CA, when I struck up a conversation with the owner. <p>&#8220;The world has changed so much,&#8221; he told me. &#8220;All my customers are online all the time, but I&#8217;m here in my shop, running the day-to-day operations. It&#8217;s hard to keep up with them.&#8221;</p>

<p><strong>Understanding the Problem</strong></p>

<p>He shared his frustrations about traditional marketing methods. &#8220;I used to rely on flyers and local ads, but they don&#8217;t bring in as many customers anymore. And using services like DoorDash? It doesn&#8217;t make sense for me because it costs $6-7 per order, eating away all of my profit. I know at least 10 other businesses around here that are struggling with the same issue.&#8221;</p>

<p>It was interesting to note that he still had a crossed-out fax number on his door for fax orders, even though it has been years since he had a fax machine. This highlighted just how much times have changed and how quickly businesses need to adapt.</p>

<p>Consumers are now driven by digital interactions, seeking instant gratification and personalized experiences. For local businesses like this Sandwich Shop, keeping up with this shift can be overwhelming.</p>
