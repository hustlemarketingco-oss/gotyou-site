<p><em>Why Local Commerce Is Coming Back — and What We’ve Learned from 6,000 Conversations This Summer</em></p>

<hr/>

<p>A few months ago, we visited a donut shop in Orange County.</p>

<p>The owner told us he was spending thousands of dollars each month on Facebook and Instagram ads. We asked, “Do you know if it’s actually bringing anyone in the door?”</p>

<p>He shook his head.</p>

<p>“I don’t know,” he said. “I ask my team, and they just tell me, ‘We need to be there.’ So I keep doing it.”</p>

<p>That stuck with us.</p>

<p>Because we’ve heard it hundreds of times since.</p>

<hr/>

<h2>We Didn’t Build GOTYOU in a Conference Room</h2>

<p>This summer, we hit the road.</p>

<p>Our team has already visited over <strong>3,000 local businesses</strong> across the Southeast — and by the time our <strong>Shop Local Summer Tour</strong> wraps in mid-August, we’ll likely have walked into <strong>another 3,000</strong>.</p>

<p>Not called. Not emailed. Not Zoomed.<br><strong>Walked in. Talked. Listened.</strong></p>

<p>We’re on the ground <strong>5 to 6 days a week</strong>, traveling town to town, city to city, by car — talking to business owners in <strong>Richmond, Virginia</strong>, <strong>Greensboro, South Carolina</strong>, and dozens of other towns across the U.S.</p>

<p>We’ve logged over <strong>2.5 million steps</strong> so far. That’s around <strong>1,250 miles</strong> — of actual, in-person conversations with barbers, bakers, boutique owners, and everyone in between.</p>

<p>You don’t get this kind of insight sitting in an office in Manhattan.<br>You only get it by showing up.</p>

<hr/>

<h2>What We’re Hearing Everywhere</h2>

<p>Here’s what’s consistent across every state, every city, every block:</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Business owners are tired.</strong> They’re spending money on ads and tools they don’t understand — and that rarely deliver results.</li>

<li><strong>They feel like they have no choice.</strong> “Everyone else is doing it” becomes the excuse to keep throwing money at Meta or Google.</li>

<li><strong>They’ve lost control of the customer relationship.</strong> They don’t own the data. They can’t track if anything is working. And they’re fed up.</li>
</ul>

<p>One restaurant owner told me he spends $3,000 a month just to “not fall behind.”<br>Another said, “We get more from our punch card than from our digital marketing.”</p>

<p>This is what happens when small businesses are handed tools designed for national brands.</p>

<hr/>

<h2>Let’s Talk About What’s Broken</h2>

<p>In 2024, over <strong>$100 billion</strong> was lost to digital ad fraud.<br>Clicks from bots. Fake traffic. “Reach” that reaches no one.</p>

<p>And yet local businesses keep pouring money into this system — because they’ve been told they need to. Even when it doesn’t work.</p>

<p>Here’s the truth no one in digital marketing wants to admit:</p>

<p><strong>No bot ever walked into a store and bought a muffin.</strong><br><strong>No bot ever ordered a burrito or got their nails done.</strong></p>

<p>Real business happens <strong>offline</strong> — in the physical world.<br>So why are we still paying for metrics that only exist online?</p>

<hr/>

<h2>O2O Isn’t a Trend. It’s a Return to Reality.</h2>

<p>What we’re talking about is <strong>Online-to-Offline commerce</strong> — using digital tools to drive real-world behavior.</p>

<p>O2O isn’t new. Groupon tried it. Foursquare tried it. But the timing wasn’t right, and the business models hurt more than they helped.</p>

<p>Now things are different:</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Consumer behavior has shifted.</strong> Nearly <strong>40% of Gen Z</strong> uses TikTok or Instagram — <em>not Google</em> — to find places to eat.</li>

<li><strong>Phones are smarter.</strong> GPS, push notifications, and AI make it easy to match digital offers with physical presence.</li>

<li><strong>Social is the new storefront.</strong> Discovery happens on your screen. But transactions still happen face-to-face.</li>
</ul>

<p>The line between online and offline isn’t a line anymore — it’s a loop.</p>
